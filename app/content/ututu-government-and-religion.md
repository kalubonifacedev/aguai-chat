# Ututu Traditional Government and Religion

## Introduction: Why Government Was Needed

In order to survive, people must organize themselves into groups and subgroups. To keep a group functioning well, they must learn to co-operate. They must try to avoid violent, ruthless struggles for wealth and power in favour of peaceful competition, and they must make and enforce rules to prevent anti-social behaviour from disrupting the group. A well-organized community is simply the practical application of methods that various people have devised to create and maintain collective orderliness and security.

It was this basic need for a well-organized community that Eze Akuma Ukwu grappled with in fashioning a Traditional Government in Ututu. Today there are nineteen distinct villages in Ututu, but this was not the case following the death of Otutu Ezema and the accession of Eze Akuma Ukwu to his father's role.

## The Original Villages

The villages that had become distinct were:

1. Amaeke Ututu
2. Amasa Ututu
3. Amodu Ututu
4. Abuma Ututu
5. Akpa Villages
6. Ohomja Ututu

## Awada Ukwu Ututu and AKPA ASAA UTUTU

These villages sent representatives to **Awada Ukwu Ututu**. Amaeke Ututu had two voting positions because the Eze Ututu resides in that village. The five other villages had one position each, which entitled them to a say in every major resolution reached at Awada Ukwu Ututu.

The holders of these **seven sacred hereditary portfolios** eventually came to be known as **AKPA ASAA UTUTU**. Because this body was also charged with fixing all the festivals in the Clan, they were also referred to as **Ndi-Etutu**.

### What was discussed at Awada Ukwu Ututu

From then on, the people of Ututu assembled at Awada Ukwu Ututu and discussed issues of common interest, such as:

- farming matters
- maintenance of law and order
- fixing traditional ceremonies in line with the agricultural cycle
- rules concerning the harvesting of new yams
- convening **Ndi-Enai** (a group of military experts) whenever there was aggression or attack from neighbouring communities

In all these matters Eze Akuma Ukwu presided. In his Court all manner of cases were settled, such as murder, poisoning, stealing and adultery.

## The Three Levels of Traditional Government

There are three levels of Traditional Government in Ututu: the Compound/Kindred level (Umunna), the Village level, and the Clan level.

### Level 1: Umunna (Compound / Kindred)

This is the compound or kindred level. This patrilineal, kin-based unit is identified with the **Nfijoku**, a set of hearthstones placed by the founder of the kindred to mark the centre of his domain.

- **Head:** the eldest man in the kindred.
- **Role:** the kindred or compound head is the eyes of his compound members, as they are his ears. He represents the compound in its external affairs with other compounds.
- **Protection:** any injury inflicted on a member of his group is considered a personal injury, for which he may seek a personal reprisal.

**How compounds were established in Ututu**
Otutu Ezema came to reside in the present Ututu with a group of warriors of whom he was the leader. Having installed himself in Amaeke Ututu by placing his hearthstone (Nkuma Etiti Ogo), the members of his group in turn placed smaller hearthstones (Nfijoku) within the area covered by the Nkuma Etiti Ogo. In Amaeke, it was the other members of Otutu Ezema's group who founded the compounds, except the **Ndi-Eziakuma compound**, which was founded by Akuma Ukwu, Otutu Ezema's first son.

**Kindreds and compounds are different**
Normally one man founds a compound, which belongs exclusively to him or his heirs. The founder could decide to bring in another person as a full member of the compound. That newcomer would then be entitled to own, on a somewhat equal basis, any property that belongs to the compound. This arrangement was not as generous as it might seem, because the newcomer usually had some special quality that endeared him to the founder, such as extraordinary physical strength, courage or resilience. So in Ututu it is customary to find one compound made up of two or more kindreds. The original founder is always given consideration: when anything in the compound is shared, the original founder receives the first share.

### Level 2: The Village

The instrument that validates the autonomy of a village in Ututu is the hearthstone **Nkuma Etiti Ogo**.

- **Okpu Abosi:** the first family to establish a village places this hearthstone and is thereafter known as "Okpu Abosi", meaning the original founder.
- **Village head:** the Chief Priest of the hearthstone is the eldest son of the founder. He presides over village meetings, which all compound members in the village (especially married men) have a right to attend and where they may air their views.
- **Cases handled:** land disputes, divorce, poisoning and other cases are settled at the house of the village head.
- **Appeals:** only matters that cannot be resolved at village level are heard at the Clan level, at Awada Ukwu Ututu, where the Eze Ututu and the members of his court, AKPA ASAA, have jurisdiction.

### Level 3: The Clan

The Clan Head is the head of Ututu Traditional Religion, so it is important to explain the religion of the Ututu people. Sociologists define religion as the aspect of culture that deals with the transcendent or supernatural, which makes religion an important part of every culture.

## Ututu Traditional Religion

The religion is often explained with a triangle diagram:

- At the apex (Y) is the **Supreme God, Obasi di relu**.
- The two ends of the base of the triangle, (X) and (Z), represent **Obasi Ututu** and **Offor Ututu** respectively.

Ututu religion recognizes one Supreme Being (God), Obasi di relu, who resides in heaven. This God, the maker of heaven and earth, can be approached through his agents (X) and (Z): Obasi Ututu and Offor Ututu.

### Obasi Ututu

Obasi Ututu has an abode at a specific spot in the Cross River which marks the boundary between Ututu and Umon (on the other side of the Cross River).

### The Eze Ututu and the Offor

The Offor holder, the Eze Ututu, is the one competent to make sacrifices to Obasi Ututu on behalf of the Ututu people. The prop for these sacrifices is the gravesite of Otutu Ezema, known as **Ihu-Izu**, which is the holiest spot in Ututu.

The Eze Ututu derives his political and religious authority from being the custodian of the **Offor Ututu**, the only symbol of Power, Peace and Rulership in Ututu.

The priestly function of the Eze Ututu as Offor holder is very demanding. He is also responsible for determining the agricultural cycle, as he is the head of Ndi-Etutu.

### The Ritual Binding of Decisions

Decisions reached at Awada Ukwu are given a ritual binding, expressed in these words:

"These laws are in accordance with our custom and must be obeyed. Those who refuse to obey them may OFFOR kill them."

### The Supreme Court of the Land

Awada Ukwu, the Court of the Eze Ututu, is the Supreme Court of the land. It settles appeals from individual villages. Its members are the **AKPA ASAA**, the holders of the seven sacred traditional portfolios.
